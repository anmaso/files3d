Folders as floors

Each folder is a floor plate. Its subfolders are rooms and its files are blocks standing in those rooms. Opening a room builds the next floor on top, and the floors you came from stay underneath, partly hidden by the one you are on. Three ways to stack them, all in a fixed isometric view, all clickable with a sample project.

Concept A

Stacked plates

Every floor has the same footprint and sits directly over its parent. Parent floors fade with depth, so you always see two or three levels of where you came from. The room you climbed out of is lit from inside. The rail on the right is a building section of the current path.

    Concept B

    Light well

    The new floor rises centred over the room you entered, joined to it by a lit shaft. Going deeper grows the tower sideways, so the path reads as a staircase of floors and the parent is less hidden.

    Concept C

    Exploded section

    Floors pull far apart and the ones below collapse to plan outlines joined by corner leaders. Only the top floor has walls and contents. Good as an overview mode that you toggle into to see the whole path at once.

    Next decisions

    Open questions

    • Huge folders

      A node_modules with 1,000+ entries cannot be 1,000 rooms. Options: one storage room with a count, a paginated wing, or rooms only for the largest N and a "more" room.

    • Layout stability

      A squarified treemap reshuffles rooms when sizes change. An ordered or strip treemap keeps rooms in place so people build spatial memory.

    • What sets room size

      Bytes, item count, or equal rooms. Bytes make media folders huge; counts favour source trees. Could be a toggle.

    • Symlinks and search

      Symlinks as bridges to another floor of the tower. Search results light up matching rooms on every visible floor at once.

    • Camera

      Fixed isometric keeps reading easy. Allow 90° rotation steps so tall blocks never hide a room, but no free orbit.

    • Renderer

      These sketches use 2D canvas with painter's ordering. The real build would use Three.js with an orthographic camera at isometric angles, for proper depth, picking and smooth transitions.
